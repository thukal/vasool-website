A field agent on a daily collection route might visit 60 to 120 borrowers a day. Every one of those visits ends the same way: the payment has to be recorded. Multiply a few extra seconds of friction by a hundred visits, across a team of twenty agents, and you get hours of lost time — and a lot of entries that quietly slip through.

This is where **voice entry** changes the maths.

## The problem with typing in the field

Manual entry looks fine in a demo and falls apart on the ground:

- Agents are standing in the sun, often on a bike, sometimes in the rain.
- Small keyboards and fiddly dropdowns slow everything down.
- Under time pressure, agents defer entry to the evening — and memory is a poor ledger.

The result is a gap between **cash collected** and **cash recorded**. That gap is where reconciliation headaches and disputes live.

## What voice entry actually does

With [voice-entry collection](/voice-entry-collection-app), the agent simply says the amount out loud. The app captures it, matches it to the account, and logs the entry with a timestamp and location.

| | Manual entry | Voice entry |
|---|---|---|
| Time per entry | 20–40 seconds | 3–5 seconds |
| Works one-handed | Rarely | Yes |
| Entry deferred to evening | Common | Rare |
| Language friction | High | Low (multi-language) |

Because it's fast, agents record **at the point of collection** instead of hours later. That single behaviour change is what tightens your books.

## Multi-language matters more than people think

India's collection workforce isn't monolingual. An agent who thinks in Tamil, Hindi, or Telugu shouldn't have to translate a number into English before recording it. Voice entry that understands multiple languages removes a real cognitive tax — and the errors that come with it.

## It's not either/or

Voice entry doesn't replace the ledger — it feeds it. Every spoken entry lands in the same [central dashboard](/staff-tools) your managers already use, with the same GPS and photo proof as any other collection. Agents who prefer typing can still type. The point is to remove friction, not add a rule.

---

For a small team, manual entry is survivable. For a growing field operation, the seconds add up into real leakage. Voice entry is the difference between recording collections *as they happen* and reconstructing them from memory at 9pm.
