Ask most daily finance offices where their book actually lives and the answer is a computer in the corner. The **daily collection software** runs on that one machine. Agents carry a companion app, and every evening someone exports from the phones and imports into the PC so the day book will tally.

It works. Thousands of lines are run this way. But it quietly builds a habit that gets expensive as you grow: **there are two copies of the truth, and neither is right until someone remembers to sync them.**

If you're comparing daily collection software right now, this is the question underneath every feature list.

## 1. The real question isn't features. It's where the ledger lives

Every product will show you route lists, receipts, and a day book. Those are table stakes. The difference that shows up in month three is *where the authoritative number sits*:

- **Desktop model** — the truth is on the office PC. The phone holds a temporary copy that must be exported, carried back, and imported.
- **Cloud model** — the truth is on the server. The phone holds a working copy that reconciles itself the moment there's signal, with no human step in between.

One of those has a person in the middle of it. People forget, fall sick, and go on leave.

## 2. What the nightly import actually costs

The sync step looks like five minutes of admin. In practice it's where the leaks are:

1. **Your defaulter list is always a day old.** If a borrower paid at 11am, the office doesn't know until the evening import. Anyone calling that borrower before then is working from yesterday.
2. **An agent who doesn't come to the office doesn't exist.** Late finish, a route on the other side of the district, a phone left at home — and that day's collections sit unrecorded.
3. **Double entry creeps back in.** When the import fails or a phone is missing, someone types the entries in from a notebook. You now have a second manual ledger, which is what the software was meant to remove.
4. **One machine is one point of failure.** A dead hard disk, a stolen laptop, or a Windows reinstall can take years of loan history with it. Ask any office that has been through it.
5. **Two branches means two books.** The desktop model doesn't extend across locations without someone emailing files around — and you can't see a group total in real time.

> The cost of the office-PC model isn't the software licence. It's that every number you look at is as fresh as the last time somebody did the import.

## 3. "Online" does not mean "needs signal"

This is where most lenders stop, and the concern is fair: *my agents collect in streets with no network — surely offline software on a PC is safer than something online?*

That would be true if online meant "needs a live connection at the doorstep." It doesn't. A properly built [daily collection app](/daily-collection-app) records entries on the phone whether or not there's coverage, and pushes them up as soon as the signal returns — no export, no cable, no import.

The distinction worth holding onto:

- **Offline-first** describes what the *agent's phone* does on a weak-signal street. This is non-negotiable for field collections.
- **Cloud** describes where the *office's* copy lives, so the dashboard is current and no single computer holds the only ledger.

You want both. The desktop model gives you offline and calls it safety, while leaving the office blind until evening. Test any product the way you'd test a bad route: put the phone in aeroplane mode, record ten collections across two borrowers, come back into coverage, and check that all ten arrive intact — and that the office dashboard moved without anyone touching the PC.

## 4. Five questions worth asking a vendor

Sync-specific, and the answers separate the categories fast:

1. **If my agent never returns to the office, do today's collections still reach the day book?**
2. **Can I open my running total on my phone at 3pm, from anywhere?**
3. **If the office computer dies tomorrow, what exactly do I lose?**
4. **How do two branches see one combined book?**
5. **When I want out, can I export every borrower, loan, and transaction myself?**

That last one matters regardless of which model you choose. If you want the control the office PC gave you without the nightly import, a [self-hosted deployment](/self-hosted-loan-software) puts the database on infrastructure you own while the agents' app still syncs on its own.

## 5. Desktop and sync vs cloud and offline-first

| On a real collection day | Office PC + import | Cloud + offline-first |
|---|---|---|
| When the office sees a payment | After the evening import | Within seconds of the entry |
| Agent doesn't come to the office | Day is unrecorded | Day syncs from wherever they are |
| No network on the street | Works, held on the phone | Works, held on the phone |
| Owner checking totals from outside | Not possible | Any phone, any time |
| Office computer fails | History at risk | History is on the server |
| Second branch | A second, separate book | One book, route-wise totals |
| Recording speed at the doorstep | Type into a form | [Speak the amount](/voice-entry-collection-app) |

## 6. Moving without losing your history

The genuine reason offices stay on a desktop product is fear of losing years of accumulated loans mid-line — and that's a sensible fear. Handle it in this order:

1. Export everything from the existing system first, and keep that file.
2. Bring across **open loans only** — borrower, principal, term, instalments already paid, balance. Closed loans can be archived rather than migrated.
3. Run one route in parallel for a week: agents record in both, and you compare the two day books each evening.
4. Move the rest only once a full week ties out to the rupee.

Do it at a month boundary if you can. Nobody should be reconciling a half-migrated line.
